Oatly freight electrification model

A five-year model of a representative 5-route regional freight network (197,000 annual miles), weighing diesel against battery-electric trucks. It combines emissions accounting, discounted cash flow, fuel-price sensitivity, grid carbon intensity and a 250-mile range constraint to show when electrification makes sense operationally and financially.

Route prioritization

Routes were ranked on emissions reduction, annual operating savings, and whether they fit within a 250-mile practical range. Routes R3 and R2 came out on top for a first phase: the biggest emissions and cost benefit within range, with less risk than converting the whole fleet.

What it means

  • Electrify short-haul, high-utilization routes first.
  • Five-year payback is very sensitive to diesel prices.
  • Incentives pull parity forward.
  • The grid's carbon intensity decides the real climate benefit, so strategy has to vary by region.

Assumptions

  • Diesel: 6.5 MPG, $0.20/mile maintenance, $150K per truck
  • EV: 2.0 kWh/mile, $0.12/mile maintenance, $350K per truck plus $50K charging
  • Practical EV range: 250 miles
  • 8% discount rate, 5-year horizon

Limitations

Route data is synthesized for demonstration. The model leaves out payload variation, battery degradation and detailed tax treatment. It's a decision-support framework, not a deployment plan.
